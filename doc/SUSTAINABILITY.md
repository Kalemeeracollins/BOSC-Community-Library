# Sustainability & Funding Strategy: BOSC Community Library

To ensure the longevity of the BOSC Community Library, we have evaluated two primary Open Source economic models to determine the best fit for our mission of public-sector education.

## 1. Model Comparison

### The "Red Hat" Service-Based Model
* **Mechanism:** The software remains 100% free, but the entity sells "Enterprise-grade" support, security auditing, and managed hosting.
* **Pros:** Highly predictable revenue; aligns interests with users who need 99.9% uptime.
* **Cons:** Requires a large, highly skilled support staff and can lead to "Open Core" temptations where better features are locked behind a paywall.

### The "Foundation-Driven" Model (Recommended)
* **Mechanism:** The project is governed by a non-profit foundation (similar to the Python Software Foundation) funded by government grants, corporate memberships, and philanthropic donations.
* **Pros:** Neutrality is maintained; no profit motive to "upsell" the government. It encourages multi-agency collaboration.
* **Cons:** Relies on the continued interest of donors and grant cycles.

## 2. Our Hybrid Strategy
For the BOSC Library, we will adopt a **Foundation-led model with a Certified Service Provider ecosystem**. The Foundation will protect the core IP, while local ICT small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) can be certified to provide paid support to regional schools, stimulating the local economy.
